# The fitted potential

The tracer needs the potential of all the mass of a galaxy at the radius of its populations. The
analysis fits that potential to every particle of each Auriga snapshot with AGAMA (Vasiliev
2019), as the sum of two expansions of different geometry (`experiments.potential.fit_potential`).
This page explains what each expansion is, why the fit needs two, what each contributes to the
vertical force that confines the populations, and how the fit compares with the potential that
the snapshot itself stores.

## Two expansions

A potential is expanded on a set of basis functions whose coefficients come from the particles.
The choice of basis decides which shapes the expansion can follow with a modest number of terms.

- Multipole: the potential is a sum of spherical harmonics $Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)$, each with a
  radial function tabulated on a grid of radii. It follows a nearly round mass distribution, such
  as a dark halo, with a few harmonics. The fit keeps $l \le 8$ on 50 radii spaced
  logarithmically from 0.1 to 300 kpc (`MULTIPOLE_GRID`).
- CylSpline: the potential is a Fourier series in azimuth whose terms are two-dimensional
  splines in $(R, z)$ on a grid. It follows a flattened mass distribution, such as a thin disk,
  directly in $R$ and $z$. The fit keeps only the axisymmetric term, $m = 0$, on 50 radii from 0.1
  to 50 kpc and 50 heights from 0.02 to 10 kpc, nearly uniform near the origin and geometric
  farther out (`CYL_GRID`).

The gas and the star particles within $R < 50$ kpc and $|z| < 10$ kpc enter the CylSpline, and
every other particle, the dark matter above all, enters the Multipole. A Multipole alone could
not hold the disk: a layer 0.3 kpc thick at $R = 8$ kpc spans an angle of about 0.04 rad seen from
the center, which needs harmonics up to $l \sim \pi/0.04 \approx 80$, whereas $l \le 8$ would smear
it over a few kpc in height. Both expansions are axisymmetric, so spiral arms and bars are
averaged over azimuth. One particle sits exactly at the origin of each snapshot, the position of
its most bound particle, and both expansions leave it out.

## What each expansion holds

The first panel below draws lines of equal density of each expansion in the meridional plane, with
$R$ and $z$ on the same scale, so that a round distribution draws circles. The lines of the
CylSpline are flattened along the plane: it holds the disk, whose density falls by an order of
magnitude within a few kpc of the plane at the solar radius, and above it the stars of the stellar
halo that lie inside its box. The lines of the Multipole are nearly circles: it holds the dark halo,
whose density falls with the distance from the center. The buttons draw the nodes of either grid
over the lines: the CylSpline's are dense near the plane and the center, where the disk is, and the
Multipole's are spherical shells. The second panel reads both densities along $z$ at the median
radius of the annulus.

## The vertical force at the populations

What confines a population is the vertical force $K_z = \partial\Phi/\partial z$ at its radius,
taken at the median radius $R_\mathrm{mid}$ of the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc. The two expansions
contribute differently:

- The force of the CylSpline rises steeply through the disk and then levels off, near the
  $2\pi G \Sigma$ of a sheet of the disk's surface density seen from outside. In Au24 it is 835
  (km/s)$^2$/kpc at 1 kpc and 1417 at 3 kpc, and it slowly declines at larger heights, where the
  disk no longer looks like an infinite sheet.
- The force of the Multipole grows nearly in proportion to height, as the force of a halo whose
  density changes little over a few kpc. In Au24 it is 495 (km/s)$^2$/kpc at 1 kpc, 1293 at 3 kpc
  and 1984 at 8 kpc.

The total force therefore never stops growing within the heights of the populations, and the
potential never becomes linear in $z$. The local power $p = d\ln\Delta\Phi/d\ln z$ shows this
directly: a potential $\Delta\Phi \propto |z|^p$ gives a tracer whose height grows as
$\sigma_z^{2/p}$ ([Tracer and sheet](./tracer-and-sheet)), so $p = 2$ means $\eta = 1$ and $p = 1$
means $\eta = 2$. In Au24 $p$ falls from 1.99 at 0.2 kpc to 1.84 at 1 kpc, 1.64 at 3 kpc and 1.27 at
8 kpc. The heights of the populations grow accordingly, with $\eta$ of
<Metric name="etaRange" /> across the halos.

The circular velocity tells the same story in the plane: in Au24 the CylSpline and the Multipole
contribute about equally at $R_\mathrm{mid}$, 146 and 153 km/s of a total of 211 km/s, added in
quadrature.

## The fit against the snapshot

The snapshot stores, for every star particle, the potential it feels, with the softening of the
simulation and with all the structure that an axisymmetric fit averages away. The last panel
compares the two in slices of $|z|$ 0.1 kpc thick: the median of the fitted $\Delta\Phi$ and the
median of the snapshot's, both over the star particles of the annulus in each slice
(`potential_check`). Averaged over eight sectors of azimuth, the snapshot's potential differs from
the fit by <Metric name="residualRange" /> (km/s)$^2$ between the plane and 1 kpc, and by at most
<Metric name="residualMax" /> (km/s)$^2$ up to <Metric name="residualTop" /> kpc. The tracer in the
potential of the snapshot is <Metric name="snapshotRange" /> times the tracer in the fit
([Tracer or sheet](../status/tracer-vs-sheet)).
