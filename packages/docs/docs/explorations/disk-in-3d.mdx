# A disk in three dimensions

Each point is one star particle, drawn in the frame of its disk, whose spin axis is
vertical. The 80,000 particles of each halo are a random sample of those within
$R < 30$ kpc and $|z| < 10$ kpc. A point has the color of its age on the scale of the
key, and overlapping points add their light.

<StarField />

The controls on the view move the camera. Drag to turn the disk, or fly to a face-on,
tilted or edge-on view with the buttons in the upper right corner. The distance slider
moves the camera along its line of sight. The halo menu loads another of the seven
disks. The bar in the lower left corner gives the length scale: in a perspective view a
length on screen depends on its depth, so the bar holds at the depth of the center of
the disk.

The panel under the view chooses the ages shown. Drag across the age key to keep the
star particles within a range of ages, or pick one of the five age groups of the
rendered images, so that one mono-age population can be seen at a time.

## Reproducing the sample

```bash
uv run experiments auriga render-data 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
```

The sample of each halo goes to `assets/data/auriga/` as a binary of int16 positions (pc)
and uint16 ages (Myr). Its JSON description also carries the age palette, so that this
page and the rendered images share one set of colors.

## Data

The disks come from the Auriga Project public data release (Grand et al. 2024). The
[acknowledgments](../acknowledgments) give the citations and the acknowledgment that the
release asks for.
