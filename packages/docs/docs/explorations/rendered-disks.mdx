# Rendered disks

These images show the stars of the seven Auriga disks of Palicio et al. (2025) at
$z = 0$, face-on and edge-on, in the frame of each disk and within 25 kpc of its center.
The Python package draws them from the star particles of the snapshots.

<GalaxyGallery />

The brightness of a pixel follows its stellar surface density, on a logarithmic scale
that spans three decades below the densest pixels. Its color is the mass-weighted mean
age of its stars, on the scale of the key, where each of the five age groups counts at a
representative age of 1, 3, 5, 7 and 11 Gyr. Faint pixels are smoothed more than bright
ones, which removes the particle noise of the outskirts without blurring the center. The
edge-on view covers $|z| < 7.5$ kpc.

## Why these seven halos

The public data release of the Auriga Project holds 30 halos of the Original suite at
Level 4, the resolution used here, and 6 at Level 3, besides suites of lower mass
and runs without baryons ([data release](https://wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg.de/auriga/data.html)).
We analyze the seven Auriga halos among the simulations of Palicio et al. (2025, Table 1):
Au2, Au3, Au16, Au19, Au23, Au24 and Au27. Their paper does not say which of its 23
galaxies enter the fit of the $J_R$-$h_Z$ relation, which leaves out outliers by clipping
(their Fig. 9). Taking the same seven lets each halo
keep the windows $Z_\mathrm{lim}$ and $L_\mathrm{kpc}$ of their Table 1, so that our heights
can be compared with theirs directly ([Fixed-window bias](../status/window-bias),
[Across galaxies](../status/across-galaxies)). No property of the halos entered the choice,
whose only purpose is that comparison.

Palicio et al. chose their cosmological disks, these seven included, by eye: "These
simulations represent Milky Way-like galaxies and were selected for the pronounced spiral
arms shown in their pre-visualisation images" (Sect. 2.1). The criterion has no stated
threshold, nor does the paper give a reason for leaving out the other 23 halos of the
suite. The seven are therefore disks with visible spiral structure, not a random or
complete sample of the Auriga galaxies. Au23 is likely barred, because its overdensity map
"suggests the presence of a long bar in the central regions" (their Sect. 3.2). The tests
of these pages hold for these seven halos. The other 23 have not been analyzed, although
the same commands would analyze them, with windows given by `--z-lim` and `--l-kpc`
because Palicio et al. list none for them.

## Reproducing the images

```bash
uv run experiments auriga render-data 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
uv run experiments auriga render 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
```

`render-data` reads each snapshot, rotates it into the frame of its disk and writes the
maps of the five age groups to `assets/data/auriga/`. `render` draws the portraits from
those maps into `assets/figures/` as JPEG, which is several times smaller than PNG for
these smooth images. The portraits are drawn for this site only.

## Data

The disks come from the Auriga Project public data release (Grand et al. 2024). The
[acknowledgments](../acknowledgments) give the citations and the acknowledgment that the
release asks for.
