# Overview

This section records how far the analysis in this repository supports the central claim
of the paper, and which parts of the claim remain untested. Every result quoted on these
pages is computed in the browser from the records that the Python package writes under
`assets/data/`. A result that the package has not produced is therefore not quoted,
whatever other evidence for it may exist.

## The claim

The paper argues that a mono-age population of a disk behaves as an isothermal tracer of
the total gravitational potential, with the vertical density

$$
\rho(z) \propto \exp\left[-\frac{\Delta\Phi(R, z)}{\sigma_z^2}\right], \qquad
\Delta\Phi(R, z) = \Phi(R, z) - \Phi(R, 0),
$$

rather than as a self-gravitating isothermal sheet of surface density $\Sigma$, whose scale
height is $h_Z = \sigma_z^2/(\pi G \Sigma)$. At fixed $\Sigma$, the sheet makes every
population follow $h_Z \propto \sigma_z^2$. A tracer follows $h_Z \propto \sigma_z^\eta$
instead, where $\eta$ depends on the shape of $\Delta\Phi$ over the heights that the
population reaches: $\eta = 1$ where $\Delta\Phi \propto z^2$, as near the midplane of any
smooth mass distribution, and $\eta = 2$ where $\Delta\Phi \propto |z|$, as above a thin
one. Because the slope $a = J_{R,\mathrm{med}}/h_Z$ of the $J_R$-$h_Z$ relation (Palicio
et al. 2025) divides by $h_Z$, the two pictures also predict different age dependences of
$a$.

We test the claim on the seven Auriga halos of Table 1 of Palicio et al. (2025) at
$z = 0$ ([why these seven](../explorations/rendered-disks#why-these-seven-halos)), with the star particles of the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc split into ten bins of
1 Gyr in age, from 0 to 10 Gyr. [Tracer or sheet](./tracer-vs-sheet) describes the
measurement and the two predictions, whereas [Fixed-window bias](./window-bias) tests the
estimator of $h_Z$ on profiles of known shape.

## Is the claim supported?

For the Auriga populations over 0-10 Gyr, the answer is yes. The tracer predicts $h_Z$
within 10 percent in <Metric name="tracerWithin10" /> of the <Metric name="populations" />
populations, whereas the sheet does so in <Metric name="sheetWithin10" />. The tracer also
reproduces how $h_Z$, and hence $a$, changes with age: the trend of $h_\mathrm{tracer}/h_Z$
with age lies within <Metric name="trendMax0" /> standard errors of 0 in every halo,
whereas that of the sheet lies at least <Metric name="sheetTrendMin" /> standard errors
from 0 in every halo and range of ages.

Over 2-10 Gyr, the trend of the tracer is negative in <Metric name="trendNegative2" />
halos, and more than 2 standard errors from 0 in <Metric name="trendOutside2" />. The
vertical Jeans equation removes that pattern when each population enters with the second
moment of its velocities measured at every height, rather than as one isothermal component
with a clipped midplane dispersion.

In the Milky Way, star counts of Gaia giants split into ages by LAMOST and by APOGEE give
the same answer. The tracer predicts $h_Z$ with ratios of
<MwMetric name="lamostTracer" /> and <MwMetric name="apogeeTracer" />, and its trend with age
over 0-10 Gyr is <MwMetric name="lamostTracerTrend0" /> and
<MwMetric name="apogeeTracerTrend0" />, whereas that of the sheet is
<MwMetric name="lamostSheetTrend0" /> and <MwMetric name="apogeeSheetTrend0" />.

Three parts of the claim are not yet established. The old populations of Au19 fall short
even of the Jeans heights (open issue 1 below). No model of this analysis turns the
relation within a galaxy into the one across galaxies (open issue 2). The tracer exceeds
the window-free heights of Imig et al. (2025) by factors of <MwMetric name="imigRatio" />,
because their heights are thinner than the star counts (open issue 3).

### Open

<Claim status="open" title="1. The old populations of Au19">

With the dispersions measured at every height, the Jeans heights of the Au19 populations
older than 5 Gyr are <Metric name="au19JeansOld" /> times their measured $h_Z$, as short as
the isothermal tracer, whose ratios are <Metric name="au19Old" />. The Jeans heights leave
out the tilt term of the vertical Jeans equation, which we measure only from three radial
bins of one annulus. With it, the heights scatter too much to decide whether it holds these
populations up: they lie within 10 percent of the measured $h_Z$ in only
<Metric name="jeansWithin10" /> of the <Metric name="populations" /> populations. Neither the
tilt term nor a departure from equilibrium has been tested as the cause.

</Claim>

<Claim status="open" title="2. Whether the relation within a galaxy predicts the one across galaxies">

Palicio et al. (2025) relate the radial action of each galaxy to the $h_Z$ of its whole
disk, whereas this analysis relates $J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ to $h_Z$ population by population,
within one annulus of each galaxy. The two compare different quantities, and no model of
this analysis turns the first into the second. See
[Across galaxies](./across-galaxies).

</Claim>

<Claim status="open" title="3. The window-free heights of Imig et al. are thinner than the star counts">

Imig et al. (2025) fit the populations of APOGEE DR17 over the whole disk with its
selection function, without a window of $|z|$. Pooled over $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ in each of
their age bins, their $h_Z$ is <MwMetric name="imigHz7" />, whereas the star counts give
<MwMetric name="lamostHz68" /> with LAMOST and <MwMetric name="apogeeHz68" /> with APOGEE at
6-8 Gyr. The tracer therefore exceeds their heights by factors of
<MwMetric name="imigRatio" />, with a trend of <MwMetric name="imigTrend" />. Four differences
between the measurements could explain it, none of them tested: their ages come from
another catalog, their populations are weighted by mass rather than by counts of giants,
their scale height flares with radius, and their bins of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ are pooled here. See [Milky Way](./milky-way#the-heights-of-imig-et-al).

</Claim>
