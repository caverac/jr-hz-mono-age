# Overview

This section records how far the analysis in this repository supports the central claim
of the paper, and which parts of the claim remain untested. Every result quoted on these
pages is computed in the browser from the records that the Python package writes under
`assets/data/`. A result that the package has not produced is therefore not quoted,
whatever other evidence for it may exist.

## The claim

The paper argues that a mono-age population of a disk behaves as an isothermal tracer of
the total gravitational potential, with the vertical density

$$
\rho(z) \propto \exp\left[-\frac{\Delta\Phi(R, z)}{\sigma_z^2}\right], \qquad
\Delta\Phi(R, z) = \Phi(R, z) - \Phi(R, 0),
$$

rather than as a self-gravitating isothermal sheet of surface density $\Sigma$, whose scale
height is $h_Z = \sigma_z^2/(\pi G \Sigma)$. At fixed $\Sigma$, the sheet makes every
population follow $h_Z \propto \sigma_z^2$. A tracer follows $h_Z \propto \sigma_z^\eta$
instead, where $\eta$ depends on the shape of $\Delta\Phi$ over the heights that the
population reaches: $\eta = 1$ where $\Delta\Phi \propto z^2$, as near the midplane of any
smooth mass distribution, and $\eta = 2$ where $\Delta\Phi \propto |z|$, as above a thin
one. Because the slope $a = J_{R,\mathrm{med}}/h_Z$ of the $J_R$-$h_Z$ relation (Palicio
et al. 2025) divides by $h_Z$, the two pictures also predict different age dependences of
$a$.

We test the claim on the seven Auriga halos of Table 1 of Palicio et al. (2025) at
$z = 0$ ([why these seven](../explorations/rendered-disks#why-these-seven-halos)), with the star particles of the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc split into ten bins of
1 Gyr in age, from 0 to 10 Gyr. [Tracer or sheet](./tracer-vs-sheet) describes the
measurement and the two predictions, whereas [Fixed-window bias](./window-bias) tests the
estimator of $h_Z$ on profiles of known shape.

## Is the claim supported?

For the Auriga populations over 0-10 Gyr, the answer is yes. The tracer predicts $h_Z$
within 10 percent in <Metric name="tracerWithin10" /> of the <Metric name="populations" />
populations, whereas the sheet does so in <Metric name="sheetWithin10" />. The tracer also
reproduces how $h_Z$, and hence $a$, changes with age: the trend of $h_\mathrm{tracer}/h_Z$
with age lies within <Metric name="trendMax0" /> standard errors of 0 in every halo,
whereas that of the sheet lies at least <Metric name="sheetTrendMin" /> standard errors
from 0 in every halo and range of ages.

Over 2-10 Gyr, the trend of the tracer is negative in <Metric name="trendNegative2" />
halos, and more than 2 standard errors from 0 in <Metric name="trendOutside2" />. The
vertical Jeans equation removes that pattern when each population enters with the second
moment of its velocities measured at every height, rather than as one isothermal component
with a clipped midplane dispersion.

In the Milky Way, star counts of Gaia giants split into ages by LAMOST, and by APOGEE on
the astroNN and the distmass age scales, give the same answer. The tracer predicts $h_Z$
with ratios of <MwMetric name="lamostTracer" />, <MwMetric name="apogeeTracer" /> and
<MwMetric name="distmassTracer" />, and its trend with age over 0-10 Gyr is
<MwMetric name="lamostTracerTrend0" />, <MwMetric name="apogeeTracerTrend0" /> and
<MwMetric name="distmassTracerTrend0" />, whereas that of the sheet is
<MwMetric name="lamostSheetTrend0" />, <MwMetric name="apogeeSheetTrend0" /> and
<MwMetric name="distmassSheetTrend0" />.

The old populations of Au19, whose tracer heights fall short of the measured ones, are held
up by the tilt term of the vertical Jeans equation, which the tracer leaves out by
construction. With it, their Jeans heights are <Metric name="au19JeansTiltOld" /> times the
measured ones. Summed over rings and ages, the tracers of the populations predict
<Metric name="sumMixtureScaled" /> of the whole-disk $h_Z$ of each halo in the
self-consistent window. With these heights, the slope of $J_{R,w}$ against $h_Z$ across the
halos is <JrMetric name="slopeScaledMixture" />, against <JrMetric name="slopeScaled" /> with
the measured ones, so the heights of the relation across galaxies follow from the tracers
within each disk, although its radial actions do not.

One part of the claim is not yet established. The heights of Imig et al. (2025) are the
scales of the exponentials that fit their populations best, which for an isothermal
population are thinner than its windowed $h_Z$, by factors of
<MwMetric name="estimatorRatios" /> in the potential of McMillan (2017). Fit the same way
and through the same counts, our star counts over their model have a median of
<MwMetric name="cellsExpMedian" /> population by population, and the logarithm of this
ratio changes by <MwMetric name="cellsExpTrend" /> per dex of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ at fixed age,
so the two measurements agree in level and in metallicity. The tracer of each population's
own $\sigma_z$ follows our windowed counts in both, with trends of
<MwMetric name="cellsTracerTrendAge" /> per ln age and <MwMetric name="cellsTracerTrend" />
per dex. The two measurements disagree in how the heights grow with age: at fixed
$[\mathrm{M/H}]$, our exponential fits over theirs change by
<MwMetric name="cellsExpTrendAge" /> per ln age. Over their pooled heights the tracer has a
trend of <MwMetric name="imigMassTrend" />, against <MwMetric name="countsDistmassTrend" />
over our counts. In the Milky Way the claim thus
holds against the star counts, whereas against the heights of Imig et al. it depends on why
their heights grow more slowly with age (open issue below).

<Claim status="open" title="Why the heights of Imig et al. grow more slowly with age than the star counts">

Fit with the same exponential and through the same counts, our counts over the model of
Imig et al. change by <MwMetric name="cellsExpTrendAge" /> per ln age at fixed
$[\mathrm{M/H}]$, from <MwMetric name="cellsExpYoung" /> below 2.51 Gyr to
<MwMetric name="cellsExpOld" /> above 5.01 Gyr, whereas the tracer over our counts changes by
<MwMetric name="cellsTracerTrendAge" />. The LAMOST counts, with their own targeting and

of our counts at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc. For the tracer, the exponential fit to the whole profile

thinner.
Two departures from their model could make their young heights thicker than ours, neither
of them tested: a young population whose profile at the Sun differs from the one that their
model, with one exponential at every radius and a linear flare, ties to the other radii,
and stars of older ages, mixed into a young bin by the errors of the ages, that reach
heights beyond our counts. Because the tracer agrees with the counts, the claim holds in the
Milky Way unless the counts, rather than their fit, misplace how the heights grow with age.
See [Milky Way](./milky-way#how-their-heights-grow-with-age).

</Claim>
