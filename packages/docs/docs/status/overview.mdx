# Overview

This section records how far the analysis in this repository supports the central claim
of the paper, and which parts of the claim remain untested. Every result quoted on these
pages is computed in the browser from the records that the Python package writes under
`assets/data/`. A result that the package has not produced is therefore not quoted,
whatever other evidence for it may exist.

## The claim

The paper argues that a mono-age population of a disk behaves as an isothermal tracer of
the total gravitational potential, with the vertical density

$$
\rho(z) \propto \exp\left[-\frac{\Delta\Phi(R, z)}{\sigma_z^2}\right], \qquad
\Delta\Phi(R, z) = \Phi(R, z) - \Phi(R, 0),
$$

rather than as a self-gravitating isothermal sheet of surface density $\Sigma$, whose scale
height is $h_Z = \sigma_z^2/(\pi G \Sigma)$. At fixed $\Sigma$, the sheet makes every
population follow $h_Z \propto \sigma_z^2$. A tracer follows $h_Z \propto \sigma_z^\eta$
instead, where $\eta$ depends on the shape of $\Delta\Phi$ over the heights that the
population reaches: $\eta = 1$ where $\Delta\Phi \propto z^2$, as near the midplane of any
smooth mass distribution, and $\eta = 2$ where $\Delta\Phi \propto |z|$, as above a thin
one. Because the slope $a = J_{R,\mathrm{med}}/h_Z$ of the $J_R$-$h_Z$ relation (Palicio
et al. 2025) divides by $h_Z$, the two pictures also predict different age dependences of
$a$.

We test the claim on the seven Auriga halos of Table 1 of Palicio et al. (2025) at
$z = 0$, with the star particles of the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc split into ten bins of
1 Gyr in age, from 0 to 10 Gyr. [Tracer or sheet](./tracer-vs-sheet) describes the
measurement and the two predictions, whereas [Fixed-window bias](./window-bias) tests the
estimator of $h_Z$ on profiles of known shape.

## Is the claim supported?

For the Auriga populations over 0-10 Gyr, the answer is yes. The tracer predicts $h_Z$
within 10 percent in <Metric name="tracerWithin10" /> of the <Metric name="populations" />
populations, whereas the sheet does so in <Metric name="sheetWithin10" />. The tracer also
reproduces how $h_Z$, and hence $a$, changes with age: the trend of $h_\mathrm{tracer}/h_Z$
with age lies within <Metric name="trendMax0" /> standard errors of 0 in every halo,
whereas that of the sheet lies at least <Metric name="sheetTrendMin" /> standard errors
from 0 in every halo and range of ages.

Two parts of the claim are not yet established. Over 2-10 Gyr, the trend of the tracer is
negative in <Metric name="trendNegative2" /> halos, and more than 2 standard errors from 0
in <Metric name="trendOutside2" />, which open issue 1 below records. The Milky Way, the
second test of the paper, has not been ported to this package, so these pages say nothing
about it.

### Open and preliminary

<Claim status="open" title="1. The oldest populations">

Over 2-10 Gyr, the trend of the tracer lies more than 2 standard errors from 0 in
<Metric name="trendOutside2" />. Over that range, <Metric name="trendNegative2" /> trends
are negative, with a median of <Metric name="trendMedian2" />. If the trends of the seven
halos scattered symmetrically about 0, a split of signs this unequal would occur with a
probability of <Metric name="trendSignTest2" />, so the tracer may fall slightly short of
the thickness of the oldest populations in every halo. In Au19, whose shortfall is the
largest, the populations older than 5 Gyr have tracer ratios of
<Metric name="au19Old" />. One hypothesis is that the radial terms of the vertical Jeans
equation, which the tracer neglects by taking $\Delta\Phi$ at a single radius, matter for
the thick old populations. It has not been tested.

</Claim>

<Claim status="preliminary" title="2. Au23: the fitted and the snapshot potentials disagree">

At the median radius of the annulus of Au23, $\Delta\Phi$ of the snapshot rises above that
of the axisymmetric fit by <Metric name="au23Residual" /> $(\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}})^2$ at
$z = 1$ kpc, whereas in the other six halos the difference stays within
<Metric name="otherResidual" /> $(\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}})^2$ at that height. The tracer in the
snapshot's potential is therefore <Metric name="au23Snapshot" /> times the tracer in the
fit. A check outside this package indicates that the snapshot's median over a slice of
height is dominated by the stars crowded along the bar of Au23, in which case the fit is
the right potential to use. Until that check is a command of the package, the tracer of
Au23 rests on the fitted potential without an independent test.

</Claim>

<Claim status="open" title="3. The link to the relation of Palicio et al. across galaxies">

Palicio et al. (2025) relate $J_R$ to $h_Z$ across galaxies, with one $h_Z$ per galaxy
fitted in its fixed windows (Sect. 3.4), whereas this analysis relates
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ to $h_Z$ population by population, within one annulus of each galaxy.
Whether the relation within a galaxy, together with the bias of the fixed windows, explains
the relation across galaxies has not been tested.

</Claim>

<Claim status="open" title="4. The Milky Way">

The paper also applies the tracer to mono-age populations of the Milky Way. None of that
analysis is part of this package yet, so these pages make no statement about the Milky
Way.

</Claim>
