# Fixed-window bias

The scale height of a profile that is not exponential depends on the window of $|z|$ in
which it is fit. With profiles of known shape, we find that a fixed window recovers
exponentials within <Metric name="exponentialFixedBias" /> of their scale, whereas it returns
<Metric name="sech2Thin" /> times the self-consistent $h_Z$ of a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layer that
is thin against the window and <Metric name="sech2Window" /> times that of one as thick as
the window. The Auriga populations follow the $\mathrm{sech}^2$ curve, as tracers in a
potential that is harmonic near the plane should. The self-consistent window that this
analysis uses recovers every injected profile within <Metric name="selfConsistentBias" />.
The bias matters wherever heights from different windows are compared, as with the
whole-disk heights of Palicio et al. (2025, Sect. 3.4), whose fixed windows have
$Z_\mathrm{lim}$ from <Metric name="zLimRange" /> kpc in the seven halos (their Table 1)
([Across galaxies](./across-galaxies)).

That the fitted height of a disk depends on the window is known. A $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layer
is close to a Gaussian at the plane and to an exponential above it (van der Kruit & Searle
1981); in edge-on galaxies, fits over different ranges of height differ by up to about 30
percent, which de Grijs & van der Kruit (1996) attribute to dust and a thick disk; fits to
simulated mono-age populations are restricted to heights above 200 pc because their profiles
flatten near the plane (Martig, Minchev & Flynn 2014a); and isothermal mono-abundance
populations are expected to have larger heights near the plane (Bovy et al. 2016). Heights
at which half of the mass lies avoid the window altogether (Ludlow et al. 2021; Martig et
al. 2026). What this page adds is the size of the bias as a function of the window, and a
window that scales with each population.

## Why the shape matters

Within a window $[0, L]$, the estimator of [Tracer or sheet](./tracer-vs-sheet#measurement)
returns the scale of the exponential truncated at $L$ whose mean height matches that of
the stars. For an exponential profile, the inverse of that scale is the maximum-likelihood
estimate of its rate, which exists when the mean height lies below $L/2$ (Deemer & Votaw
1955, Sect. 3) and is consistent whatever the window, although its scatter grows as the
window shrinks relative to the scale. For any other profile the
result depends on the window, so there is no single true scale height to recover. We
therefore define the reference $h_\mathrm{ref}$ of a profile as its self-consistent
$h_Z$, the quantity that both the measurements and the predictions of this analysis use,
and call bias the departure of the fixed-window estimate from it.

A $\mathrm{sech}^2(z/z_0)$ layer, the profile of a self-gravitating isothermal population,
is flat for $|z| \ll z_0$ and falls as $\exp(-2|z|/z_0)$ for $|z| \gg z_0$. A window much
wider than $z_0$ holds the whole profile, whose mean height is
$z_0 \ln 2 \approx 0.69\,z_0$, so the fit returns a scale close to that. A window narrower
than $z_0$ holds an almost flat profile, which the fit reads as a very large scale, or as
no scale at all when the mean height inside the window approaches $L/2$. The
self-consistent window lies between these limits, which is why the fixed window can err in
either direction.

## The injection test

The command draws <Metric name="injectionDraws" /> samples of
<Metric name="injectionStars" /> heights from each of three families of profiles. The
families are an exponential, a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layer, and a mixture in which 70 percent
of the stars follow an exponential of scale $h$ and 30 percent one of scale $3h$, which is
cusped at the plane. Each sample is fitted in a fixed window with $Z_\mathrm{lim} = 1$ kpc
and in the self-consistent window with $k = 2$. The reference of each profile is computed
on a fine grid of heights without noise. Because the profiles and the estimators scale
with length, the ratios depend only on $h_\mathrm{ref}/Z_\mathrm{lim}$.

```bash
uv run experiments window-injection
```

For exponentials, the median of the fixed window stays within
<Metric name="exponentialFixedBias" /> of $h_\mathrm{ref}$ up to
$h_\mathrm{ref} = 3\,Z_\mathrm{lim}$, as the consistency of the maximum-likelihood estimate
requires, although its scatter grows: at the thickest exponential the 16th to 84th
percentiles span <Metric name="exponentialSpreadWide" />. The $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layers are
underestimated when thin and overestimated when thick, with ratios of
<Metric name="sech2Thin" /> at $h_\mathrm{ref} \approx 0.2\,Z_\mathrm{lim}$ and
<Metric name="sech2Window" /> at $h_\mathrm{ref} \approx Z_\mathrm{lim}$. The mixture errs
the other way when thin, with a ratio of <Metric name="mixtureThin" /> for the thinnest
mixture, because the window then holds its thick component in full.

The fixed fit has no solution in up to <Metric name="fixedFailedMax" /> of the draws of the
thickest profiles, so the medians of those profiles describe only the draws that have one,
presumably those whose noise makes the profile fall inside the window. This is the likely reason why the $\mathrm{sech}^2$ curve turns down at the
largest scales. The self-consistent window recovers every profile within
<Metric name="selfConsistentBias" />. This agreement holds by construction for the exact
profiles, because the reference uses the same estimator, so the bottom panel measures only
the effect of drawing <Metric name="injectionStars" /> stars.

## The Auriga populations

Each Auriga population enters the diagram with its self-consistent $h_Z$ in place of
$h_\mathrm{ref}$ and the $Z_\mathrm{lim}$ of its halo. The table compares, in ranges of
$h_Z/Z_\mathrm{lim}$, the median ratio of the populations with the median of each injected
curve evaluated at the same populations, interpolated in $\log$-$\log$. A population beyond
the range of a curve is left out of the median of that curve. Below
$h_Z = 0.5\,Z_\mathrm{lim}$, the populations give <Metric name="windowLowHalos" />, against
<Metric name="windowLowSech2" /> for $\mathrm{sech}^2$ and
<Metric name="windowLowExponential" /> for the exponential. Between 0.5 and 1 they give
<Metric name="windowMidHalos" />, against <Metric name="windowMidSech2" /> and
<Metric name="windowMidExponential" />.

Below $h_Z = Z_\mathrm{lim}$ the populations thus behave like $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layers, as
expected of profiles that are flat near the plane, which an isothermal tracer is wherever
$\Delta\Phi$ is harmonic. Between 1 and 2 they give <Metric name="windowHighHalos" />,
between the <Metric name="windowHighExponential" /> of the exponential and the
<Metric name="windowHighSech2" /> of $\mathrm{sech}^2$. This suggests that the thicker
populations are less flat at the plane than a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layer relative to the
window, which we have not tested with the profiles of the populations themselves.

The bias does not enter the comparison of [Tracer or sheet](./tracer-vs-sheet), because
the measured and the predicted $h_Z$ are both fitted in the self-consistent window. It
does enter any comparison with the heights of Palicio et al. (2025): a population with
$h_Z$ between 0.5 and 2 $Z_\mathrm{lim}$ is overestimated in a fixed window by a median
factor of <Metric name="windowMidHalos" /> to <Metric name="windowHighHalos" />.

## Limitations

None of the three families is fitted to the populations, so the agreement with the
$\mathrm{sech}^2$ curve is a statement about medians in coarse ranges of
$h_Z/Z_\mathrm{lim}$, not about individual profiles. The ratios of the populations divide
by their self-consistent $h_Z$, which carries the finite-sample error that the bottom
panel shows for the injected profiles. Finally, the whole-disk heights of Palicio et al.
mix populations of all ages. For them the overview gives the ratio of the fixed-window to
the self-consistent $h_Z$, <Metric name="wholePalicio" />, but no injection test of mixed
populations has been run.
