# Across galaxies

Summed over rings of 1 kpc and ages of 1 Gyr, the isothermal tracers of the populations of
each Auriga disk predict <Metric name="sumMixtureScaled" /> of the scale height of the whole
disk in the self-consistent window. Across the seven halos, the slope of a proxy for the
radial action of Palicio et al. (2025, Sect. 3.4) against these predicted heights is
<JrMetric name="slopeScaledMixture" />, against <JrMetric name="slopeScaled" /> with the
measured heights and <JrMetric name="palicioSlope" /> km/s in Palicio et al., so the heights
of the relation between radial action and scale height across galaxies follow from the
tracers within each disk. The slope depends on the window of the heights: in the fixed
windows of Palicio et al. it is <JrMetric name="slopePalicio" />. The radial actions
themselves lie outside the tracer, which says nothing about $J_R$.

Palicio et al. (2025) relate the radial action of each galaxy to the scale height of its
whole disk, across 23 simulated galaxies, whereas the populations of this analysis relate
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ to $h_Z$ within one annulus of one galaxy. This page builds a
reproducible stand-in for their radial action, measures the relation in the seven Auriga
halos, and sums the tracers of each disk into its whole-disk height.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments auriga jr-map 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments auriga tracer-sum 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
uv run experiments auriga jr-relation 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
```

## A proxy for their radial action

Palicio et al. describe their radial action as "an individual reference value,
$J_{R,w}$, that separates the high and low radial action regions" of the face-on map of
the median $J_R$ of each galaxy, which they read from the map rather than compute with a
stated rule. Their paper lists neither the value for each galaxy nor the solar angular
momentum $L_\odot$ in which they quote the slope, $3.69\times10^{-2}\,L_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}$.
We therefore define a proxy that follows the construction of their maps as far as the
paper describes it (`experiments.jr_map`). The star particles with $|Z| < Z_\mathrm{lim}$
inside the square $|X|, |Y| < L_\mathrm{kpc}$ fill cells of 0.375 kpc, their cell width.
Each cell with at least 10 particles takes the median $J_R$ of its particles, weighted by
stellar mass as they weight it. The proxy is the median of the cell values
whose centers lie at $0.2\,L_\mathrm{kpc} \le R < L_\mathrm{kpc}$, the radial range of
their $h_Z$, so that half of those cells lie above it and half below. Unlike their maps,
the cells are not smoothed with an Epanechnikov kernel of 0.5 kpc. Our $J_R$ come from the
Staeckel fudge of AGAMA in the potential of `experiments.potential`, not from their fit and
code. With $L_\odot = R_0V_0$, $R_0 = 8.2$ kpc and $V_0 = 233$ km/s, the value
that the companion project
[`jr-hz-relation`](https://github.com/caverac/jr-hz-relation) adopts, their slope is
<JrMetric name="palicioSlope" /> km/s.

The whole-disk $h_Z$ of each halo comes from `experiments auriga analyze`, once in their
windows, averaging the exponential fits within $|Z| < Z_\mathrm{lim}$ and
$Z_\mathrm{lim}/2$ over $0.2\,L_\mathrm{kpc} \le R < L_\mathrm{kpc}$, and once in the
self-consistent window $L = 2h_Z$ over the same radii.

because the proxy is not their $J_{R,w}$, these numbers neither confirm nor refute their
relation. The two slopes do differ by more than the error of either, which suggests that a
relation across galaxies measured with fixed windows depends on how far each disk extends
beyond its window.

## The whole disk as a sum of tracers

The relation across galaxies uses the $h_Z$ of all the stars of a disk, whereas the tracer
predicts the profile of one mono-age population. If every population of a disk is an
isothermal tracer, the profile of the whole disk is their sum, whose $h_Z$ follows from
the kinematics of its populations and the potential alone (`experiments.tracer_sum`). We
split the radial range of Palicio et al. of each halo into rings of 1 kpc, and each ring
into age bins of 1 Gyr from 0 to 14 Gyr. Each cell enters as the tracer
$\exp(-\Delta\Phi/\sigma_z^2)$ of its clipped $\sigma_z$ within 0.3 kpc of the plane, in
the fitted potential at the center of its ring, with the current mass of its stars, which
is how Palicio et al. weight their histograms. A cell with fewer than 30 stars within
0.3 kpc of the plane has no $\sigma_z$ and is left out, which removes at most
<Metric name="sumUnmodeledMax" /> of the mass of a disk. For comparison, the same sum with
one tracer per ring, from the $\sigma_z$ of all its stars, keeps the change of the
potential and of $\sigma_z$ with radius but not the mixture of ages. Both sums are fitted
like the star particles, in the window of Palicio et al. and in the self-consistent one,
with errors from 50 bootstrap draws of the stars.

predictions are <Metric name="sumSinglePalicio" /> and <Metric name="sumMixturePalicio" />

<Metric name="au19ZLim" /> kpc of Au19 against its whole-disk $h_Z$ of
<Metric name="au19WholePalicio" /> kpc, measures the curvature of the profile near the
plane, which a fit with a fixed window turns into large changes of $h_Z$
([Window bias](./window-bias)).

With the predicted $h_Z$ in the self-consistent window, the slope of $J_{R,w}$ against
$h_Z$ is <JrMetric name="slopeScaledMixture" />, which lies
<JrMetric name="scaledMixtureShift" /> standard errors from the slope with the measured
$h_Z$ and <JrMetric name="scaledMixtureDeviation" /> from that of Palicio et al. With the
predicted $h_Z$ in their windows, it is <JrMetric name="slopePalicioMixture" />. The heights
of the relation across galaxies therefore follow from the tracers of the populations of
each disk, in the window in which the tracer was tested. The relation also needs the
radial action of each disk, which the tracer does not predict. The companion project takes
it from the epicycle, $J_R^{\max} = c_{\max}\sigma_R^2/\kappa$, which makes the slope a
ratio of radial to vertical heating. We measure that ratio population by population, as
$a = J_{R,\mathrm{med}}/h_Z$, but have not compared $J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ with
$\sigma_R^2/\kappa$.

## Limitations

The sum of tracers inherits the assumptions of the tracer: each cell takes its $\sigma_z$
from the plane and its $\Delta\Phi$ from the axisymmetric fit at a single radius. Its
errors come from resampling the stars, not from the fitted potential.

The proxy differs from their $J_{R,w}$ in three ways that we cannot remove: their value is
read from each map rather than computed, their maps are smoothed with a kernel of 0.5 kpc,
and their $J_R$ come from their own potential fits and code. The fit uses seven of their 23
galaxies, by ordinary least squares, whereas theirs clips outliers iteratively, keeping in
each step only the points whose discrepancy lies within the 75th percentile. Their slope
in km/s assumes the $L_\odot$ of the companion project, which their paper does not state.
