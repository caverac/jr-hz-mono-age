# Across galaxies

Palicio et al. (2025, Sect. 3.4) relate the radial action of each galaxy to the scale
height of its whole disk, across 23 simulated galaxies, whereas the populations of this
analysis relate $J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ to $h_Z$ within one annulus of one galaxy. This page
measures their relation in the seven Auriga halos with a reproducible stand-in for their
radial action, and asks how much of it the window of their scale heights sets.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments auriga jr-map 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
uv run experiments auriga jr-relation 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
```

## A proxy for their radial action

Palicio et al. describe their radial action as "an individual reference value,
$J_{R,w}$, that separates the high and low radial action regions" of the face-on map of
the median $J_R$ of each galaxy, which they read from the map rather than compute with a
stated rule. Their paper lists neither the value for each galaxy nor the solar angular
momentum $L_\odot$ in which they quote the slope, $3.69\times10^{-2}\,L_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}$.
We therefore define a proxy that follows the construction of their maps as far as the
paper describes it (`experiments.jr_map`). The star particles with $|Z| < Z_\mathrm{lim}$
inside the square $|X|, |Y| < L_\mathrm{kpc}$ fill cells of 0.375 kpc, their cell width,
and each cell with at least 10 particles takes the median $J_R$ of its particles,
weighted by stellar mass as they weight it. The proxy is the median of the cell values
whose centers lie at $0.2\,L_\mathrm{kpc} \le R < L_\mathrm{kpc}$, the radial range of
their $h_Z$, so that half of those cells lie above it and half below. Unlike their maps,
the cells are not smoothed with an Epanechnikov kernel of 0.5 kpc, and $J_R$ comes from
the Staeckel fudge of AGAMA in the potential of `experiments.potential`, not from their
fit and code. With $L_\odot = R_0V_0$, $R_0 = 8.2$ kpc and $V_0 = 233$ km/s, the value
that the companion project
[`jr-hz-relation`](https://github.com/caverac/jr-hz-relation) adopts, their slope is
<JrMetric name="palicioSlope" /> km/s.

The whole-disk $h_Z$ of each halo comes from `experiments auriga analyze`, once in their
windows, averaging the exponential fits within $|Z| < Z_\mathrm{lim}$ and
$Z_\mathrm{lim}/2$ over $0.2\,L_\mathrm{kpc} \le R < L_\mathrm{kpc}$, and once in the
self-consistent window $L = 2h_Z$ over the same radii.

Because seven halos leave both slopes uncertain by a large fraction of their value, and
because the proxy is not their $J_{R,w}$, these numbers neither confirm nor refute their
relation. The two
slopes do differ by more than the error of either, which suggests that a relation across
galaxies measured with fixed windows depends on how far each disk extends beyond its
window. Whether the relation within a galaxy, population by population, predicts the one
across galaxies is still untested. The two compare different quantities: the
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ of one population of one annulus, and a statistic of the map of $J_R$
of the whole disk.

## Limitations

The proxy differs from their $J_{R,w}$ in three ways that we cannot remove: their value is
read from each map rather than computed, their maps are smoothed with a kernel of 0.5 kpc,
and their $J_R$ come from their own potential fits and code. The fit uses seven of their 23
galaxies, by ordinary least squares, whereas theirs clips outliers iteratively, keeping in
each step only the points whose discrepancy lies within the 75th percentile. Their slope
in km/s assumes the $L_\odot$ of the companion project, which their paper does not state.
