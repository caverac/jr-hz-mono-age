# Across galaxies

Summed over rings of 1 kpc and ages of 1 Gyr, the isothermal tracers of the populations of
each Auriga disk predict <Metric name="sumMixtureScaled" /> of the scale height of the whole
disk in the self-consistent window. Across the seven halos, the slope of a proxy for the
radial action of Palicio et al. (2025, Sect. 3.4) against these predicted heights is
<JrMetric name="slopeScaledMixture" />, against <JrMetric name="slopeScaled" /> with the
measured heights and <JrMetric name="palicioSlope" /> km/s in Palicio et al., so the heights
of the relation between radial action and scale height across galaxies follow from the
tracers within each disk. The agreement with the slope of Palicio et al. rests on one halo,
<JrMetric name="influentialHalo" />, without which the slope is
<JrMetric name="withoutInfluentialScaled" />. The slope also depends on the window of the
heights: in the fixed windows of Palicio et al. it is <JrMetric name="slopePalicio" />. The
radial actions themselves lie outside the tracer, which says nothing about $J_R$.

Palicio et al. (2025) relate the radial action of each galaxy to the scale height of its
whole disk, across 23 simulated galaxies, whereas the populations of this analysis relate
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ to $h_Z$ within one annulus of one galaxy. This page builds a
reproducible stand-in for their radial action, measures the relation in the seven Auriga
halos, and sums the tracers of each disk into its whole-disk height.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments auriga jr-map 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments auriga tracer-sum 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
uv run experiments auriga jr-relation 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
```

## A proxy for their radial action

Palicio et al. describe their radial action as "an individual reference value, $J_{R,w}$,
that separates the high and low radial action regions" of the face-on map of the median
$J_R$ of each galaxy, which they read from the map rather than compute with a stated rule.
Their paper does not list the value for each galaxy. Their Fig. 9 gives the line
$J_{R,w} = 3.69\times10^{-2}\,h_Z + 2.95\times10^{-3}$, with $J_{R,w}$ in units of the solar
angular momentum $L_\odot$ and $h_Z$ in kpc. We therefore define a proxy that follows the
construction of their maps as far as the paper describes it (`experiments.jr_map`). The star
particles with $|Z| < Z_\mathrm{lim}$ inside the square $|X|, |Y| < L_\mathrm{kpc}$ fill
cells of 0.375 kpc, their cell width. Each cell with at least 10 particles takes the median
$J_R$ of its particles, weighted by stellar mass as they weight it. The proxy is the median
of the cell values whose centers lie at $0.2\,L_\mathrm{kpc} \le R < L_\mathrm{kpc}$, the
radial range of their $h_Z$, so that half of those cells lie above it and half below. Unlike
their maps, the cells are not smoothed with an Epanechnikov kernel of 0.5 kpc. Our $J_R$
come from the Staeckel fudge of AGAMA in the potential of `experiments.potential`, not from
their fit and code. Their 2025 paper does not restate $L_\odot$; Palicio et al. (2023, Sect.
3) express actions in $L_\odot = R_\odot V_\odot$, with the Sun at $R_\odot = 8.249$ kpc and
a circular velocity there of 238.5 km/s. In those units their line has a slope of
<JrMetric name="palicioSlope" /> km/s and an intercept of
<JrMetric name="palicioIntercept" /> kpc km/s.

The whole-disk $h_Z$ of each halo comes from `experiments auriga analyze`, once in their
windows, averaging the exponential fits within $|Z| < Z_\mathrm{lim}$ and
$Z_\mathrm{lim}/2$ over $0.2\,L_\mathrm{kpc} \le R < L_\mathrm{kpc}$, and once in the
self-consistent window $L = 2h_Z$ over the same radii.

## The whole disk as a sum of tracers

The relation across galaxies uses the $h_Z$ of all the stars of a disk, whereas the tracer
predicts the profile of one mono-age population. If every population of a disk is an
isothermal tracer, the profile of the whole disk is their sum, whose $h_Z$ follows from
the kinematics of its populations and the potential alone (`experiments.tracer_sum`).
Models of the Milky Way disk build it the same way, from isothermal components of
different ages in the total potential, with dispersions prescribed by an assumed relation
between age and dispersion (Sch&ouml;nrich & Binney 2009; Binney 2010; Sysoliatina & Just
2022; Robin et al. 2003, 2022). Here the dispersions are measured, and the sum is tested
against heights measured independently. We
split the radial range of Palicio et al. of each halo into rings of 1 kpc, and each ring
into age bins of 1 Gyr from 0 to 14 Gyr. Each cell enters as the tracer
$\exp(-\Delta\Phi/\sigma_z^2)$ of its clipped $\sigma_z$ within 0.3 kpc of the plane, in
the fitted potential at the center of its ring, with the current mass of its stars, which
is how Palicio et al. weight their histograms. A cell with fewer than 30 stars within
0.3 kpc of the plane has no $\sigma_z$ and is left out, which removes at most
<Metric name="sumUnmodeledMax" /> of the mass of a disk. For comparison, the same sum with
one tracer per ring, from the $\sigma_z$ of all its stars, keeps the change of the
potential and of $\sigma_z$ with radius but not the mixture of ages. Both sums are fitted
like the star particles, in the window of Palicio et al. and in the self-consistent one,
with errors from 50 bootstrap draws of the stars.

is <JrMetric name="slopeScaledMixture" />, which lies <JrMetric name="scaledMixtureShift" />
standard errors from the slope with the measured $h_Z$ and
<JrMetric name="scaledMixtureDeviation" /> from that of Palicio et al. With the predicted
$h_Z$ in their windows, it is <JrMetric name="slopePalicioMixture" />. Without
<JrMetric name="influentialHalo" />, the slope with the predicted heights is
<JrMetric name="withoutInfluentialMixture" />, and whichever halo is left out it stays
within <JrMetric name="leaveOneOutMixtureShift" /> standard errors of the slope with the
measured heights. The heights of the relation across galaxies therefore follow from the
tracers of the populations of each disk, in the window in which the tracer was tested. The
relation also needs the radial action of each disk, which the tracer does not predict. The
companion project takes it from the epicycle, $J_R^{\max} = c_{\max}\sigma_R^2/\kappa$,
which makes the slope a ratio of radial to vertical heating, as Jia et al. (2023) read their
relation between radial action and scale height in the Milky Way, and as van der Kruit & de
Grijs (1999) related the ratio of vertical to radial dispersions to the flattening of disks
across galaxies. We measure that ratio population by population, as
$a = J_{R,\mathrm{med}}/h_Z$, but have not compared $J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ with
$\sigma_R^2/\kappa$.

## Limitations

The sum of tracers inherits the assumptions of the tracer: each cell takes its $\sigma_z$
from the plane and its $\Delta\Phi$ from the axisymmetric fit at a single radius. Its
errors come from resampling the stars, not from the fitted potential.

The proxy differs from their $J_{R,w}$ in three ways that we cannot remove: their value is
read from each map rather than computed, their maps are smoothed with a kernel of 0.5 kpc,
and their $J_R$ come from their own potential fits and code. The fit uses seven of their 23
galaxies, by ordinary least squares, whereas theirs clips outliers iteratively, keeping in
each step only the points whose discrepancy lies within the 75th percentile. Their line
in km/s assumes the $L_\odot$ of Palicio et al. (2023), which their 2025 paper does not
restate.
