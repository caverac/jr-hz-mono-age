# Tracer or sheet

This page compares the scale heights that the isothermal tracer and the self-gravitating
sheet predict for the <Metric name="populations" /> mono-age populations of the seven
Auriga halos with the scale heights we measure. It then compares the age dependence of the
slope $a = J_{R,\mathrm{med}}/h_Z$ that follows from each prediction with the measured one.

## Measurement

For each halo, the analysis fits an axisymmetric potential to all the particles of the
snapshot with AGAMA (Vasiliev 2019), as a CylSpline expansion for the gas and the stars of
the disk region and a Multipole expansion for everything else (`experiments.potential`).
The star particles of the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc are split into ten bins of 1 Gyr in
age. In each bin, $\sigma_z$ is the dispersion of $v_z$ of the stars with $|z| < 0.3$ kpc,
clipped at 4 standard deviations estimated from the median absolute deviation.
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ is the median radial action of the stars with $|z| < 0.5$ kpc,
computed in the fitted potential with the Staeckel fudge of AGAMA
(`experiments.populations`).

We fit $h_Z$ to the heights $|z|$ of the stars, weighted by their initial mass, by matching
their mean height inside a window $|z| < L$ to that of an exponential truncated at $L$,
which for an exponential profile is the maximum-likelihood estimate of its scale (Deemer &
Votaw 1955). The estimate is averaged with the same fit inside $L/2$. The window scales
with the population, $L = k\,h_Z$ with $k = 2$, taking the smallest $L$ that satisfies the
condition (`experiments.heights`). Errors are half the range between the 16th and the 84th
percentiles of 300 bootstrap resamplings of the stars of each bin. Each resampling
recomputes every quantity at once, so that ratios of quantities measured on the same
stars, such as $a$ and $h_\mathrm{pred}/h_Z$, carry their correlations.

The sheet predicts $h_Z = \sigma_z^2/(\pi G \Sigma)$, with $\Sigma$ the mass per area of
all the particles of the annulus with $|z| < 1.1$ kpc. The tracer is the profile
$\exp(-\Delta\Phi/\sigma_z^2)$, with $\Delta\Phi$ from the fitted potential at the median
radius of the annulus. It passes through the same self-consistent window as the stars, so
that the measured and the predicted $h_Z$ come from one estimator. A second tracer uses the potential of the snapshot, averaged over azimuth, in place of the
fit, as a check of the fit.

## Age dependence

We fit $\beta_a$, the exponent of $a \propto \tau^{\beta_a}$, by weighted least squares of
$\ln a$ against $\ln \tau$ over the bins of 0-10 Gyr and of 2-10 Gyr, for the measured $a$
and for $a$ with each predicted $h_Z$ in place of the measured one. These exponents
describe how $a$ changes with age, but the difference between two of them is not a test
of a prediction. Each fit is weighted by its own errors, and $\ln a$ is not a straight line
in $\ln \tau$: the measured $\beta_a$ changes by <Metric name="exponentShift" /> when the
bins of 0-2 Gyr are left out. Two fits with different weights can therefore differ even
where the measured and the predicted $a$ agree bin by bin. The two values of $a$ also share
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$, whose error would enter twice in their difference.

The test is instead the trend of the ratio $h_\mathrm{pred}/h_Z$, the exponent $p$ of
$h_\mathrm{pred}/h_Z \propto \tau^p$ fitted by weighted least squares over the same bins,
with the bootstrap errors of the ratios as weights (`experiments.populations`).
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ cancels in the ratio, and the fit uses one set of weights. With those
weights, the trend equals the measured minus the predicted $\beta_a$, so a prediction that
reproduces how $a$ changes with age has a trend of 0. Where the ratios scatter about a power
law by more than their errors, the error of the trend is multiplied by $\sqrt{\chi^2_\nu}$,
the root of the $\chi^2$ per degree of freedom of the fit, so that the scatter widens the
error instead of sharpening the test.

## The vertical Jeans equation

The isothermal tracer solves the vertical Jeans equation of a steady, axisymmetric
population,

$$
\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial (R\nu\sigma_{Rz})}{\partial R} + \frac{\partial (\nu\sigma_z^2)}{\partial z}
= -\nu\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial z},
$$

under two assumptions: $\sigma_z$ does not change with height, and the first term, the tilt
term, vanishes. A population of 1 Gyr of ages mixes stars of several dispersions, so its
$\sigma_z$ can grow with height and its velocities can have heavier tails than one
Gaussian. We measure, in slices of $|z|$ up to 12 kpc, the variance of $v_z$ of each
population weighted by initial mass, without clipping, because the equation needs the
second moment of all its stars. The slices reach that high because the self-consistent
windows of the oldest populations of Au19 reach 8 kpc, and the equation holds $\sigma_z^2$
and the tilt term constant above the last slice.

The tilt term needs the radial derivative of $\nu\sigma_{Rz}$, which the 1.5 kpc of the
annulus measure poorly. We take it from the stars of each population over
$6 < R < 10.5$ kpc, a range centered on the annulus, in rings of 0.5 kpc. In each slice,
straight lines of $\ln\nu$ and of $\sigma_{Rz} = \langle v_R v_z\,\mathrm{sign}(z)\rangle$
against $R$ give

$$
t = \frac{1}{R\nu}\frac{\partial (R\nu\sigma_{Rz})}{\partial R}
= \sigma_{Rz}\left(\frac{1}{R} + \frac{\partial \ln\nu}{\partial R}\right) + \frac{\partial \sigma_{Rz}}{\partial R}
$$

at $R_\mathrm{mid}$, where the sign of $z$ folds the two sides of the plane, on which
$\sigma_{Rz}$ has opposite signs. A slice needs 50 stars in every ring, so that the lines
sample $R_\mathrm{mid}$ symmetrically; otherwise its tilt term counts as 0. With the tilt
term written as $\nu t(z)$, the equation integrates to

$$
\nu(z) = \frac{\sigma_z^2(0)}{\sigma_z^2(z)}
\exp\left[-\int_0^z \frac{\partial_{z'}\Phi + t}{\sigma_z^2}\,dz'\right],
$$

which goes through the same self-consistent window as the stars, without and with $t$
(`experiments.jeans`). The profile is a diagnostic of the tracer's assumptions, not a
prediction, because it uses the kinematics measured at every height. Figure 1 shows both
profiles with the buttons "Jeans, $\sigma_z(z)$" and "Jeans, $\sigma_z(z)$ and tilt".

## The steady state

The Jeans equation, and the tracer with it, assume a steady disk that is symmetric about
its plane. A disk that a satellite or a bar has disturbed departs from both, and its
populations can be thicker or thinner than their kinematics imply. We measure four
departures in the stars of each population inside its self-consistent window
(`experiments.equilibrium`): the thickening rate
$\langle \mathrm{sign}(z)\,v_z\rangle/\langle|z|\rangle$, which is
$d\ln\langle|z|\rangle/dt$ for stars that stay in the annulus, the bending velocity
$\langle v_z\rangle$, the north-south asymmetry of mass $(M_+ - M_-)/(M_+ + M_-)$, and the
amplitude of the $m = 1$ term of the mean $z$ over eight sectors of azimuth, a warp. All
four vanish in a steady disk that is axisymmetric and symmetric about its plane. Their
errors come from 300 bootstrap draws of the stars. The warp, a modulus, is positive even
without one, so its noise floor is about its error.

```bash
uv run experiments auriga equilibrium 2 3 16 19 23 24 27
```

## Limitations

Both predictions take $\sigma_z$ from the stars within 0.3 kpc of the plane and assume
that it does not change with height, an assumption whose cost the Jeans heights above
measure. That $\sigma_z$ is clipped at 4 standard deviations, which removes the tails of a
population that mixes several dispersions, so it is smaller than the second moment of the
population; how much this moves each tracer height on its own has not been measured. The tracer also takes
$\Delta\Phi$ at a single radius, which neglects the radial terms of the vertical Jeans
equation, the tilt term that the Jeans heights above measure. That tilt term comes from
straight lines over 4.5 kpc of radius, which cannot follow a curvature of $\nu\sigma_{Rz}$
with radius, and its bootstrap errors do not include that. Its potential is an axisymmetric fit, which cannot
follow a bar, although averaged over azimuth the snapshot's potential agrees with it
([overview](./overview)). The bootstrap errors of the predictions carry those of
$\sigma_z$, but not those of the fitted potential, whose accuracy we know only on the mock
galaxy of the overview. The trend tests a power law in age, so a prediction that fails in
a few bins without a trend, as a bump in the ratios would, shows up in $\chi^2_\nu$ rather
than in the trend. Finally, every population comes from one annulus per halo, so these
results say nothing yet about how $\eta$ depends on radius.
