# Milky Way

This page tests the isothermal tracer on the mono-age populations of the Milky Way, whose
scale heights come from star counts rather than from star particles, and compares it with
the heights that Imig et al. (2025) fit without a window of $|z|$. The measurement follows
the one of the Auriga disks as closely as the data allow, so that the two tests can be
read side by side.

```bash
uv run experiments milky-way fetch
uv run experiments milky-way check
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments milky-way analyze
```

## Measurement

A spectroscopic survey cannot give the density of a population against $|z|$ without its
selection function, whereas a complete photometric sample can. We count the Gaia DR3
giants of a cylinder of radius 1.5 kpc around the Sun, within $|z| < 1.2$ kpc, whose
reddening-free Wesenheit absolute magnitude
$M_W = G - 1.9\,(G_\mathrm{BP} - G_\mathrm{RP}) + 5\log_{10}(\varpi/\mathrm{mas}) - 10$
lies in a window, and split each slice of $|z|$ into ages with the fraction of its
spectroscopic stars that belong to the population (`experiments.mw_counts`). Two surveys
give the ages. The primary red-clump stars of LAMOST (Huang et al. 2020) split the
<MwMetric name="lamostGaia" /> Gaia stars within 0.3 mag of the red-clump peak of $M_W$,
through <MwMetric name="lamostMatched" /> matches by position and $G$. APOGEE DR17, with
the ages of the astroNN catalog, splits the <MwMetric name="apogeeGaia" /> giants of the
box $-2.6 < M_W < -0.9$ through <MwMetric name="apogeeMatched" /> matches by Gaia source
identifier. Both surveys select their targets by magnitude and color, not by age at a
fixed position, so the fraction of a slice is unbiased although their completeness changes
with $|z|$.

A population is a range of ages of the low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ disk with
$[\mathrm{Fe/H}] > -0.6$, in the counts and in the kinematics of both surveys
(`experiments.mw_catalogs`). We fit $h_Z$ to the counts in the self-consistent window
$L = 2h_Z$, as for the Auriga populations; because the counts stop at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc, a
population whose window would reach beyond it has no $h_Z$. The kinematics come from the
giants of the solar annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc: $\sigma_z$ from those with
$|z| < 0.3$ kpc, with their median velocity error removed in quadrature, and
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ from those with $|z| < 0.5$ kpc, in the potential of McMillan (2017).
Every star is placed in the Galactocentric frame of the astroNN catalog, with the Sun at
$R_0 = $ <MwMetric name="r0" /> kpc and 20.8 pc above the plane (Bennett & Bovy 2019).

The tracer is the profile $\exp(-\Delta\Phi/\sigma_z^2)$ of McMillan (2017) at $R_0$, and
the sheet uses the mass of the same potential within $|z| < 1.1$ kpc,
$\Sigma = $ <MwMetric name="sigmaSurf" /> $M_\odot\,\mathrm{pc}^{-2}$, as the Auriga sheet
uses the mass of all particles in the same layer. Both go through the self-consistent
window. The errors of the ratios come from <MwMetric name="nBootstrap" /> bootstrap draws
that resample, in each draw, the Gaia stars, their matches and the kinematic stars of the
population.

## The heights of Imig et al.

Imig et al. (2025) fit every population of APOGEE DR17 in bins of
$[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$, $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ and age with an exponential vertical profile over
the whole footprint of the survey, with its selection function, so their heights carry no
window of $|z|$. We pool their low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$ populations with
$[\mathrm{M/H}] > -0.6$ in each of their age bins up to 10 Gyr, weighting each by its
surface density at the solar radius, and fit the pooled profile in the self-consistent
window (`experiments.mw_imig`). Their ages come from the distmass catalog, whereas the
$\sigma_z$ of the tracer comes from the APOGEE stars with astroNN ages in the same range,
so the comparison mixes two age scales.

a scale height that flares linearly with radius, and their bins of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ are
pooled here into one population.

## Limitations

The age fraction of a slice assumes that the spectroscopic targeting does not depend on
age at a fixed position, which holds for selections in magnitude and color but has not been
checked against the targeting flags. The counts stop at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc, so the window of
the oldest populations, about twice their $h_Z$, comes close to the edge of the counts. The
kinematic samples cover the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc, whereas the counts cover the
cylinder of radius 1.5 kpc around the Sun. Ages carry typical errors of 30 percent for the
LAMOST red clump (Huang et al. 2020), with our cut at 50 percent, and up to 40 percent by
our cut for APOGEE, so bins of 2 Gyr mix their neighbors. The potential of McMillan (2017) enters without its errors, and
the clipped $\sigma_z$ of the tracer removes the tails of populations that mix several
dispersions, as in the Auriga disks.
