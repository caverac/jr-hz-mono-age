# Milky Way

We test the isothermal tracer on the mono-age populations of the Milky Way, whose scale
heights we measure from star counts of Gaia giants around the Sun. With the ages of LAMOST,
and of APOGEE on two age scales, the tracer in the potential of McMillan (2017) predicts
the scale height of every population of 2 Gyr, with ratios of
<MwMetric name="lamostTracer" />, <MwMetric name="apogeeTracer" /> and
<MwMetric name="distmassTracer" /> and no trend with age, so the populations of the Milky
Way behave as those of the Auriga disks do. The heights that Imig et al. (2025) fit to
APOGEE with a model of the whole disk agree with ours in level and in their dependence on
metallicity once both are measured with the same estimator, whereas they grow more slowly
with age, a difference that our tests of the data that both analyses share do not explain.

```bash
uv run experiments milky-way fetch
uv run experiments milky-way check
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments milky-way analyze
```

## Star counts

A spectroscopic survey cannot give the density of a population against $|z|$ without its
selection function, whereas a complete photometric sample can. We count the Gaia DR3
giants of a cylinder of radius 1.5 kpc around the Sun, within $|z| < 1.2$ kpc, whose
reddening-free Wesenheit absolute magnitude
$M_W = G - 1.9\,(G_\mathrm{BP} - G_\mathrm{RP}) + 5\log_{10}(\varpi/\mathrm{mas}) - 10$
lies in a window, and split each slice of $|z|$ into ages with the fraction of its
spectroscopic stars that belong to the population (`experiments.mw_counts`). Two surveys
give the ages, one of them on two age scales. The primary red-clump stars of LAMOST
(Huang et al. 2020) split the <MwMetric name="lamostGaia" /> Gaia stars within 0.3 mag of
the red-clump peak of $M_W$, through <MwMetric name="lamostMatched" /> matches by position
and $G$. APOGEE DR17 splits the <MwMetric name="apogeeGaia" /> giants of the box
$-2.6 < M_W < -0.9$ through matches by Gaia source identifier, with the ages of the
astroNN catalog (<MwMetric name="apogeeMatched" /> matches) and with those of the distmass
catalog of Stone-Martinez et al. (2024), which Imig et al. (2025) adopt
(<MwMetric name="distmassMatched" /> matches). Both surveys select their targets by
magnitude and color, not by age at a fixed position, so the fraction of a population is
unbiased although their completeness changes with $|z|$.

A population is a range of ages of the low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ disk with
$[\mathrm{Fe/H}] > -0.6$, in the counts and in the kinematics of both surveys
(`experiments.mw_catalogs`), on each age scale. We fit $h_Z$ to the counts in the
self-consistent window $L = 2h_Z$, as for the Auriga populations, so a population whose
window would reach beyond the edge of the counts at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc has no $h_Z$. The
kinematics come from the giants of the solar annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc: $\sigma_z$ from
those with $|z| < 0.3$ kpc, with their median velocity error removed in quadrature, and
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ from those with $|z| < 0.5$ kpc, in the potential of McMillan (2017).
Every star is placed in the Galactocentric frame of the astroNN catalog, with the Sun at
$R_0 = $ <MwMetric name="r0" /> kpc and 20.8 pc above the plane (Bennett & Bovy 2019).

The populations change across the cylinder, the old ones being more common toward the
Galactic center. Near the plane, a check outside the package finds most matches of both
surveys toward the anticenter, so the fraction of a population in a slice depends on where
in the slice it is measured. We model the fraction at a position
as $P(x, z) = A(z)\,e^{g x}$, with $x$ the distance from the Sun toward the Galactic center
in the plane and one gradient $g$ per population, shared by all its slices. The gradient
maximizes the Poisson likelihood of the memberships of the matches, with $A$ of each slice
profiled out. Each Gaia star then enters the population with the weight
$A(z)\,e^{g x}$ (`experiments.mw_counts.population_counts`). On synthetic populations of known gradient,
the corrected counts recover the true $h_Z$
(`tests/test_milky_way.py::test_population_counts_correct_a_gradient`). The gradients of the
age bins span <MwMetric name="gradientRange" /> kpc$^{-1}$ (see the table of scale heights),
negative below 6 Gyr and positive above.

The tracer is the profile $\exp(-\Delta\Phi/\sigma_z^2)$ of McMillan (2017) at $R_0$, with
the $\sigma_z$ of the population. For comparison with the Auriga test we also carry the
self-gravitating sheet, the vertical half of the slope of the
[companion paper](https://github.com/caverac/jr-hz-relation), with the mass of the same
potential within $|z| < 1.1$ kpc,
$\Sigma = $ <MwMetric name="sigmaSurf" /> $M_\odot\,\mathrm{pc}^{-2}$, and the profile
$\mathrm{sech}^2(|z|/z_0)$, $z_0 = \sigma_z^2/(\pi G \Sigma)$. We write $h_\mathrm{pred}$
for the height that either predicts for a population from its own $\sigma_z$. Both
predictions go through the same counts as the populations, in each bootstrap draw through the counts of the draw,
because the slices of 0.1 kpc thicken thin populations. The fraction of a population is
interpolated between the centers of the slices and held constant below the first one, so it
falls more slowly than a thin population does. With the Gaia stars of APOGEE, an
exponential of 0.15 kpc comes out <MwMetric name="biasApogee15" /> times its scale, one of
0.20 kpc <MwMetric name="biasApogee20" /> times and one of 0.40 kpc
<MwMetric name="biasApogee40" /> times (`experiments.mw_counts.count_bias`). The mass of a
predicted profile in each slice, over the Gaia stars of the slice, is its fraction, which
the interpolation and the self-consistent window then fit like a measured one
(`experiments.mw_counts.predicted_hz`). The errors come from
<MwMetric name="nBootstrap" /> bootstrap draws that resample the Gaia stars, their matches
and the kinematic stars of the population.

## The heights of Imig et al.

Imig et al. (2025) measure the same heights in a different way. They fit every population
of APOGEE DR17, in bins of $[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$, $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ and age, with a model of
the whole footprint of the survey corrected for its selection function. Their model of a
population is $\nu(R, z) = \nu_\odot \Sigma(R) \exp[-|z|/h_Z(R)]$, with $\Sigma$ a broken
exponential in $R$ and a scale height that flares linearly,
$h_Z(R) = h_{Z\odot} + A_\mathrm{flare}(R - R_\odot)$, around their Sun at
$R_\odot = 8.3$ kpc (their Section III.3). Their normalization $\nu_\odot$ is the number
density at $R_\odot$ of the giants of their sample, and their ages come from the distmass
catalog. The grids of $h_Z(R)$ in their released table pivot the flare at 8.122 kpc, whereas
we follow the text. Our counts lie around the Sun, so their model enters them averaged over
the disk of our cylinder and integrated over each slice of $|z|$, then goes through our
counts as the tracer does (`experiments.mw_imig.cylinder_slices`).

Through our counts, their heights are thinner than ours above 4 Gyr, where our counts over
their heights are <MwMetric name="imigGapOld" />, whereas below 4 Gyr they are
<MwMetric name="imigGapYoung" />. Four heights per age bin, all through our counts, separate
the ways in which the two measurements differ (`experiments.mw_imig`): their
low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$ populations with $[\mathrm{M/H}] > -0.6$ pooled by the midplane
mass density of each at $R_\odot$ and by its $\nu_\odot$, the analog of our counts of
giants, and our APOGEE counts of the same ages on the astroNN and the distmass scale.
Pooling their populations by counts rather than by mass changes their heights by factors of
<MwMetric name="imigWeighting" />. Moving our counts from the astroNN to the distmass scale
changes ours by factors of <MwMetric name="imigAgeScale" />. Neither the weighting nor the
age scale therefore accounts for the difference. At <MwMetric name="imigAges7" />, for
example, their heights are <MwMetric name="imigHz7" /> by mass and
<MwMetric name="imigCountsHz7" /> by counts, whereas our counts on their age scale give
<MwMetric name="countsDistmassHz7" />.

### Two estimators of the scale height

An exponential has a cusp at the plane, whereas an isothermal population has a core, so
once each is normalized to its sum the core falls short of the exponential at the plane and
exceeds it above. The shortfall deepens with metallicity, as it should if it comes from the
core, because at fixed age the metal-rich populations are the colder ones

made on the counts before the correction of the gradient, point away from selections that
could empty the plane instead: the shortfall persists in
smaller cylinders, with less dust along the plane, and with the fractions taken only from
matches beyond the bright limit of APOGEE. Above $[\mathrm{M/H}] = 0.1$ the two sequences
of $[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$ converge, so stars of the high-alpha sequence could fall below the
split of their Eq. (1) and thicken our metal-rich cells. A third check of the same kind
rules this out: the metal-rich matches lie well below the split at every height, and moving
the split in either direction, or replacing it with the $[\mathrm{Mg/Fe}]$ split of
astroNN, leaves the metal-rich ratio and its shortfall at the plane in place.

### The kinematics of each cell

The tracer predicts each cell from its own stars. The kinematic stars of a cell are the
APOGEE giants of its ages and $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ in the solar annulus with $|z| < 0.3$ kpc,
as in the main test. Their $\sigma_z$ gives a tracer that goes through our counts. A cell
needs `MIN_CELL_KINEMATICS` (default 30) of them for a tracer, which
<MwMetric name="cellsWithTracer" /> measured cells have, with at least
<MwMetric name="cellsKinMin" /> each.

At fixed age, $\ln \sigma_z$ falls by <MwMetric name="slopeSigmaApogee" /> per dex of
$[\mathrm{M/H}]$. Sharma et al. (2021, Table 2) fit the dispersions of LAMOST and GALAH
stars with $\sigma_z \propto 1 + \gamma_{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]}[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$ at fixed age,
angular momentum and height, with $\gamma_{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]} = -0.52 \pm 0.01$, which is the
same slope at solar metallicity. The tracer of each cell over our windowed counts has
medians of <MwMetric name="cellsTracerPoor" />, <MwMetric name="cellsTracerSolar" /> and
<MwMetric name="cellsTracerRich" /> in the three ranges of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$. Its logarithm
changes by <MwMetric name="cellsTracerTrendAge" /> per ln age and by
<MwMetric name="cellsTracerTrend" /> per dex. The slopes of $\ln h_Z$ per dex at fixed age
are <MwMetric name="slopeTracerApogee" /> for the tracer and
<MwMetric name="slopeCountsApogee" /> for our counts. The tracer thus follows our counts

### The targeting of APOGEE

The targeting limits of APOGEE do not change the cells. Our counts assume that the targeting does not depend on the population at a fixed position.
The surveys select their targets by color, however. Because the colors of giants change
with metallicity, a color limit that changes across the fields at different $|z|$ would
bias the fraction of each metallicity in the slices. We test this with the targeting that APOGEE
records for every star: the limits of $H$ and of $(J-K_s)_0$ of the cohort it was targeted
in, and its color $(J-K_s)_0 = J - K_s - 1.5A_{K_s}$, with the extinction $A_{K_s}$ of the
targeting (Zasowski et al. 2013). Our matches come from cohorts limited at
$(J-K_s)_0 > 0.5$ and at $(J-K_s)_0 > 0.3$, and some cohorts also reject stars redder than
$(J-K_s)_0 = 0.8$, the coolest and most metal-rich giants, in their fields only. We repeat
the comparison of the cells with only the matches of a box of $H$ and $(J-K_s)_0$ that the
limits of every cohort they come from contain, so that one selection holds in every field
(`experiments.mw_catalogs.TargetingBox`). The first box, <MwMetric name="boxA" />, keeps
<MwMetric name="boxMatchesA" /> of the <MwMetric name="cellsMatches" /> matches and drops the
cohorts with a red limit, whereas the second, <MwMetric name="boxB" />, keeps
<MwMetric name="boxMatchesB" /> by applying that red limit to every star.

<TargetingTable />

With one selection in every field, the medians of our windowed counts over their model rise
from <MwMetric name="boxPoorA" /> and <MwMetric name="boxPoorB" /> for the metal-poor
populations to <MwMetric name="boxRichA" /> and <MwMetric name="boxRichB" /> for the
metal-rich ones. At fixed age, their logarithms change by <MwMetric name="boxTrendA" /> and
<MwMetric name="boxTrendB" /> per dex of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, as without the boxes. A

than drawing them at random (Zasowski et al. 2013, Section 4.4), so the chance that a star
is targeted depends on its $H$ and, at a given distance, on its luminosity, which changes
with the population.

### The counts of LAMOST

The counts of LAMOST, whose targeting, metallicities and ages are independent of those of
APOGEE, change with metallicity at fixed age as the APOGEE counts do. The boxes leave the
even spacing in $H$ untested, because no limit of a cohort records it.
We test it, with every other part of the selection of APOGEE, by splitting the LAMOST
counts into the same cells (`experiments.mw_imig_cells.lamost_metallicity_counts`). A cell
then holds the LAMOST matches of its ages on the scale of Huang et al. (2020) and of its bin
of the LAMOST $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$, on the low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ sequence of LAMOST.
LAMOST selects its targets, and measures their metallicities and ages, independently of
APOGEE, so a bias of APOGEE does not carry over to these counts.

These ages are not on the distmass scale of Imig et al., so the ratio of the two heights in
a cell would compare stars of different ages. We compare instead how each height changes
with metallicity at fixed age: the slope of $\ln h_Z$ per dex of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ over the
measured cells, with one intercept per age bin
(`experiments.populations.fit_grouped_log_slope`). An offset between the age scales that is
the same at every metallicity moves the intercepts, not the slope. A zero point between the
$[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$ of LAMOST and the $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ of ASPCAP leaves the slope of the counts
unchanged, whereas a stretch scales it. The table gives the slopes of every height for every
set of cells, the APOGEE ones of the previous sections included.

### How their heights grow with age

Fit the same way, the two measurements still disagree in how $h_Z$ grows with age. At fixed
$[\mathrm{M/H}]$, the logarithm of our exponential fit over theirs changes by
<MwMetric name="cellsExpTrendAge" /> per ln age, with medians of
<MwMetric name="cellsExpYoung" /> younger than 2.51 Gyr and
<MwMetric name="cellsExpOld" /> older than 5.01 Gyr, whereas the tracer over our counts
changes by <MwMetric name="cellsTracerTrendAge" />. The pooled heights show the
same: the trend with age of the tracer over their heights is
<MwMetric name="imigMassTrend" /> by mass, against <MwMetric name="countsDistmassTrend" />
over our counts on the same age scale. Our counts and the tracer thus grow with age at the
same rate, whereas their heights grow more slowly. The table gives the trends of the three
ratios in every set of cells:

<RatioTrendTable />

The trend does not come from the selection of APOGEE: in the LAMOST cells, with another
survey's targeting and ages, our exponential fits over theirs change by
<MwMetric name="lamostExpTrendAge" /> per ln age, although these ages are not on their
scale. Part of it can come from the range of the fits. Their fit takes the stars at every
height that APOGEE reaches, whereas ours stops at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc. Because the profile of a
hot isothermal population steepens with height, a fit that reaches higher returns a thinner
exponential. For the tracer, the exponential fit to the
whole profile is <MwMetric name="estimatorWhole" /> times the fit below 1.2 kpc, the
lowest for the hottest population (see the table of estimators), so their old heights
should come out thinner than ours by up to that factor. That would account for part of the
excess of our old cells, but not for the young cells, where our fits are the thinner.

The rest is untested. Both measurements use the same stars on the same age scale, so the
errors of the ages mix the same neighbors into both, whereas the two measurements differ in
where the stars lie. Their $h_Z$ at $R_\odot$ comes from a model of the whole footprint, in
which each population has one exponential in $|z|$ at every radius and a linear flare,
whereas ours comes from the stars within 1.5 kpc of the Sun. Two departures from their
model could make their young heights thicker than ours: a young population whose profile
at the Sun differs from the one that their model ties to the other radii, and stars of
older ages, mixed into a young bin by the errors of the ages, that reach heights beyond
our counts.

## Limitations

Our heights are those that the counts measure, with the bias of the slices for thin
populations ([Star counts](#star-counts)). Every prediction goes through the same counts, so the ratios
do not carry that bias, whereas the heights in the tables do.

The fraction of a slice assumes that the spectroscopic targeting does not depend on the
population, its age or its metallicity, at a fixed position, which a selection in magnitude
and color satisfies only if its limits are the same for every field. The correction of the
gradient assumes that the fraction of a population changes across the cylinder as
$e^{g x}$, with one $g$ at every height, so a gradient that changes with height, or one
along the azimuth, stays in the counts. For APOGEE we have checked the targeting with the
limits of every star, which do not change the cells. For LAMOST we have not checked its
limits, although its counts change with metallicity at fixed age within the errors of those
of APOGEE, so a bias larger than those errors would have to be common to both surveys. If
it fails, the counts of a population are biased in the slices of $|z|$ where the targeting
changes, which would bear on the comparison with Imig et al., whose fit corrects for the
selection function.

The exponential fits to our counts stop at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc, whereas the fit of Imig et al.
has no such limit. For the tracer, the fit to the whole profile is
<MwMetric name="estimatorWhole" /> times the fit below 1.2 kpc, which the comparison of the
cells does not correct. The counts stop at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc, so the window of the oldest
populations, about twice their $h_Z$, comes close to the edge of the counts. On the
distmass scale the bin of 8-10 Gyr has no $h_Z$ in <MwMetric name="distmassFailed810" /> of
its bootstrap draws, so its error, taken from the draws whose window stays inside the
counts, is underestimated. The kinematic samples cover the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc,
whereas the counts cover the cylinder of radius 1.5 kpc around the Sun. Ages carry typical
errors of 30 percent for the LAMOST red clump (Huang et al. 2020), with our cut at 50
percent, and up to 40 percent by our cut for APOGEE on either scale, so bins of 2 Gyr mix
their neighbors. The potential of McMillan (2017) enters without its errors, and the
clipped $\sigma_z$ of the tracer removes the tails of populations that mix several
dispersions, as in the Auriga disks.
