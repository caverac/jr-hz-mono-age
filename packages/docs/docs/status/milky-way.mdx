# Milky Way

This page tests the isothermal tracer on the mono-age populations of the Milky Way, whose
scale heights come from star counts rather than from star particles, and compares it with
the heights that Imig et al. (2025) fit without a window of $|z|$. The measurement follows
the one of the Auriga disks as closely as the data allow, so that the two tests can be
read side by side.

```bash
uv run experiments milky-way fetch
uv run experiments milky-way check
OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 uv run experiments milky-way analyze
```

## Measurement

A spectroscopic survey cannot give the density of a population against $|z|$ without its
selection function, whereas a complete photometric sample can. We count the Gaia DR3
giants of a cylinder of radius 1.5 kpc around the Sun, within $|z| < 1.2$ kpc, whose
reddening-free Wesenheit absolute magnitude
$M_W = G - 1.9\,(G_\mathrm{BP} - G_\mathrm{RP}) + 5\log_{10}(\varpi/\mathrm{mas}) - 10$
lies in a window, and split each slice of $|z|$ into ages with the fraction of its
spectroscopic stars that belong to the population (`experiments.mw_counts`). Two surveys
give the ages, one of them on two age scales. The primary red-clump stars of LAMOST
(Huang et al. 2020) split the <MwMetric name="lamostGaia" /> Gaia stars within 0.3 mag of
the red-clump peak of $M_W$, through <MwMetric name="lamostMatched" /> matches by position
and $G$. APOGEE DR17 splits the <MwMetric name="apogeeGaia" /> giants of the box
$-2.6 < M_W < -0.9$ through matches by Gaia source identifier, with the ages of the
astroNN catalog (<MwMetric name="apogeeMatched" /> matches) and with those of the distmass
catalog of Stone-Martinez et al. (2024), which Imig et al. (2025) adopt
(<MwMetric name="distmassMatched" /> matches). Both surveys select their targets by magnitude and color, not by age at a
fixed position, so the fraction of a slice is unbiased although their completeness changes
with $|z|$.

A population is a range of ages of the low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ disk with
$[\mathrm{Fe/H}] > -0.6$, in the counts and in the kinematics of both surveys
(`experiments.mw_catalogs`), on each age scale. We fit $h_Z$ to the counts in the self-consistent window
$L = 2h_Z$, as for the Auriga populations; because the counts stop at $|z| = 1.2$ kpc, a
population whose window would reach beyond it has no $h_Z$. The kinematics come from the
giants of the solar annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc: $\sigma_z$ from those with
$|z| < 0.3$ kpc, with their median velocity error removed in quadrature, and
$J_{R,\mathrm{med}}$ from those with $|z| < 0.5$ kpc, in the potential of McMillan (2017).
Every star is placed in the Galactocentric frame of the astroNN catalog, with the Sun at
$R_0 = $ <MwMetric name="r0" /> kpc and 20.8 pc above the plane (Bennett & Bovy 2019).

The tracer is the profile $\exp(-\Delta\Phi/\sigma_z^2)$ of McMillan (2017) at $R_0$, and
the sheet uses the mass of the same potential within $|z| < 1.1$ kpc,
$\Sigma = $ <MwMetric name="sigmaSurf" /> $M_\odot\,\mathrm{pc}^{-2}$, as the Auriga sheet
uses the mass of all particles in the same layer. The errors of the ratios come from
<MwMetric name="nBootstrap" /> bootstrap draws that resample, in each draw, the Gaia stars,
their matches and the kinematic stars of the population.

The slices bias the counts of thin populations. The fraction of a population is
interpolated between the centers of the slices of 0.1 kpc and held constant below the first
one, so it falls more slowly than a thin population does, and the counts move its stars to
larger $|z|$. With the Gaia stars of APOGEE, an exponential of 0.15 kpc comes out
<MwMetric name="biasApogee15" /> times its scale, one of 0.20 kpc
<MwMetric name="biasApogee20" /> times and one of 0.40 kpc <MwMetric name="biasApogee40" />
times (`experiments.mw_counts.count_bias`). The predictions therefore go through the same
counts as the populations, in each bootstrap draw through the counts of the draw: the mass
of a predicted profile in each slice, over the Gaia stars of the slice, is its fraction,
which the interpolation and the self-consistent window then fit
(`experiments.mw_counts.predicted_hz`). The tracer enters with its profile, and the sheet
with its profile $\mathrm{sech}^2(|z|/z_0)$, $z_0 = \sigma_z^2/(\pi G \Sigma)$, whose height
in the window $L = 2h_Z$ is $z_0$ itself. The sheet is thus the prediction that the Auriga
test compares with the particles.

## The heights of Imig et al.

Imig et al. (2025) fit every population of APOGEE DR17 in bins of
$[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$, $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ and age with an exponential vertical profile over
the whole footprint of the survey, with its selection function, so their heights carry no
window of $|z|$. Their model of a population is
$\nu(R, z) = \nu_\odot \Sigma(R) \exp[-|z|/h_Z(R)]$, with $\Sigma$ a broken exponential in
$R$ and a scale height that flares linearly,
$h_Z(R) = h_{Z\odot} + A_\mathrm{flare}(R - R_\odot)$, around their Sun at
$R_\odot = 8.3$ kpc (their Section III.3). Their normalization $\nu_\odot$ is the number
density at $R_\odot$ of the giants of their sample, and their ages come from the distmass
catalog. The grids of $h_Z(R)$ in their table pivot the flare at 8.122 kpc instead, whereas
we follow the paper. Our counts lie around the Sun, so their model enters them averaged
over the disk of our cylinder around their Sun and integrated over each slice of $|z|$,
then goes through our counts as the tracer does (`experiments.mw_imig.cylinder_slices`).

In each of their age bins up to 10 Gyr we compare four heights, all through our counts, so
that each difference between their measurement and ours can be removed in turn
(`experiments.mw_imig`). The first two pool their low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$ populations
with $[\mathrm{M/H}] > -0.6$: by the midplane mass density of each at $R_\odot$, and by its
$\nu_\odot$, which is the analog of our counts of giants. The other two are our APOGEE
counts of the same ages, on the astroNN and on the distmass scale. The $\sigma_z$ of the
tracer comes from the APOGEE stars of the same ages on the distmass scale, theirs.

### One population at a time

The pooled heights mix the bins of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ of each age bin, and each side weights
them in its own way. We remove the pooling by comparing every population of Imig et al.
with our counts of the same cell (`experiments.mw_imig_cells`): the APOGEE stars of the
same ages on the distmass scale and of the same bin of the ASPCAP $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, below
the $[\alpha/\mathrm{M}]$ that separates the two sequences in their Eq. (1). Their model of
each population goes through our cylinder and counts, as in the pooled heights. A cell needs
<MwMetric name="cellsMin" /> matched stars for our $h_Z$, which
<MwMetric name="cellsMeasured" /> cells have, holding <MwMetric name="cellsStars" />.

Over these cells our counts are thicker than their model by a median factor of
<MwMetric name="cellsMedian" />, so the pooling does not cause the disagreement. The ratio depends on metallicity instead. Its median is
<MwMetric name="cellsMetalPoor" /> below $[\mathrm{M/H}] = -0.1$,
<MwMetric name="cellsMetalSolar" /> between $-0.1$ and $0.1$, and
<MwMetric name="cellsMetalRich" /> above $0.1$. The slope of its logarithm is
<MwMetric name="cellsTrend" /> per dex of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$. The two measurements thus
agree for the metal-poor populations, whereas they disagree for the metal-rich ones.

Whatever causes the disagreement must therefore change with metallicity at fixed age, as
two differences between the measurements could. Their $h_Z$ is a parameter of a fit over the
footprint of APOGEE out to $R = 20$ kpc, in which a single exponential in $|z|$ holds at
every radius and its scale changes only linearly with $R$, whereas ours is fit to a cylinder
of radius 1.5 kpc around the Sun within $|z| < 1.2$ kpc. Each population enters their fit
with its own radial profile and flare, so if the profile at $R_0$ is not an exponential, or
the flare not linear, the scale height that the fit returns at $R_\odot$ can be off by an
amount that differs between populations. Our counts, in turn, assume that the targeting
does not depend on the population at a fixed position. The surveys select their targets by
color, however. Because the colors of giants change with metallicity, a color limit that
changes across the fields at different $|z|$ would bias the fraction of each metallicity in
the slices.

### The targeting of APOGEE

We test the second explanation with the targeting that APOGEE records for every star: the
limits of $H$ and of $(J-K_s)_0$ of the cohort it was targeted in, and its color
$(J-K_s)_0 = J - K_s - 1.5A_{K_s}$, with the extinction $A_{K_s}$ of the targeting
(Zasowski et al. 2013). Our matches come from cohorts limited at $(J-K_s)_0 > 0.5$ and at
$(J-K_s)_0 > 0.3$, and some cohorts also reject stars redder than $(J-K_s)_0 = 0.8$, the
coolest and most metal-rich giants, in their fields only. We repeat the comparison of the
cells with only the matches of a box of $H$ and $(J-K_s)_0$ that the limits of every cohort
they come from contain, so that one selection holds in every field
(`experiments.mw_catalogs.TargetingBox`). The first box, <MwMetric name="boxA" />, keeps
<MwMetric name="boxMatchesA" /> of the <MwMetric name="cellsMatches" /> matches and drops the
cohorts with a red limit, whereas the second, <MwMetric name="boxB" />, keeps
<MwMetric name="boxMatchesB" /> by applying that red limit to every star.

### The counts of LAMOST

The boxes leave the even spacing in $H$ untested, because no limit of a cohort records it.
We test it, with every other part of the selection of APOGEE, by splitting the LAMOST
counts into the same cells (`experiments.mw_imig_cells.lamost_metallicity_counts`). A cell
then holds the LAMOST matches of its ages on the scale of Huang et al. (2020) and of its bin
of the LAMOST $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$, on the low-$[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ sequence of LAMOST.
LAMOST selects its targets, and measures their metallicities and ages, independently of
APOGEE, so a bias of APOGEE does not carry over to these counts.

These ages are not on the distmass scale of Imig et al., so the ratio of the two heights in
a cell would compare stars of different ages. We compare instead how each height changes
with metallicity at fixed age: the slope of $\ln h_Z$ per dex of $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ over the
measured cells, with one intercept per age bin
(`experiments.populations.fit_grouped_log_slope`). An offset between the age scales that is
the same at every metallicity moves the intercepts, not the slope. A zero point between the
$[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$ of LAMOST and the $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ of ASPCAP leaves the slope of the counts
unchanged, whereas a stretch scales it, so neither turns a flat slope into a steep one. The
table gives both slopes for every set of cells, the APOGEE ones of the previous sections
included.

## Limitations

Our heights are those that the counts measure, with the bias of the slices for thin
populations (see Measurement). Every prediction goes through the same counts, so the ratios
do not carry that bias, whereas the heights in the tables do.

The fraction of a slice assumes that the spectroscopic targeting does not depend on the
population, its age or its metallicity, at a fixed position, which a selection in magnitude
and color satisfies only if its limits are the same for every field. For APOGEE we have
checked this with the targeting limits of every star, which do not change the cells. For
LAMOST we have not checked its limits, although its counts change with metallicity at fixed
age within the errors of those of APOGEE, so a bias larger than those errors would have to
be common to both surveys. If it fails, the counts of a population are biased in the
slices of $|z|$ where the targeting changes, which would bear on the disagreement with
Imig et al., whose fit corrects for the selection function. The counts stop at
$|z| = 1.2$ kpc, so the window of the oldest populations, about twice their $h_Z$, comes
close to the edge of the counts. On the distmass scale the bin of 8-10 Gyr has no $h_Z$ in
<MwMetric name="distmassFailed810" /> of its bootstrap draws, so its error, taken from the
draws whose window stays inside the counts, is underestimated. The kinematic samples cover
the annulus $7.5 < R < 9$ kpc, whereas the counts cover the cylinder of radius 1.5 kpc
around the Sun. Ages carry typical errors of 30 percent for
the LAMOST red clump (Huang et al. 2020), with our cut at 50 percent, and up to 40 percent
by our cut for APOGEE on either scale, so bins of 2 Gyr mix their neighbors. The potential
of McMillan (2017) enters without its errors, and the clipped $\sigma_z$ of the tracer
removes the tails of populations that mix several dispersions, as in the Auriga disks.
